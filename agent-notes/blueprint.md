# Blueprint style (`--style plot`): what a change must keep

Read this before you change `packages/choreo/src/trace/plot-shots.ts`, `packages/render/src/plot-*.ts` or `packages/choreo/src/board-title.ts`. The root `AGENTS.md` points here.

- The blueprint is `style: 'plot'`. `choreo/src/trace/plot-shots.ts` owns its whole score: `plotScore(inp)` returns the camera shots and the `PlotScore` that `scene.ts` attaches as `tl.scene.plot`. `film.ts` hands plot frames to `drawPlotFrame` in `render/src/plot-draw.ts`. The other `plot-*.ts` files in `render/src` each draw one thing:
  - `plot-sheet.ts`: the ending (the board's name and the stamp);
  - `plot-body.ts`: part bodies and lid text;
  - `plot-object.ts`: the axonometric view;
  - `plot-plan.ts`: the plan view and the shared `Pen`;
  - `plot-signature.ts`: the signature shot;
  - `plot-stack.ts`: the layer stack.
- The film is one arc of shot kinds, in the order `ORDER[plan]` sets (`open`, `signature`, `macro`, `outline`, `tilt`, `assembly`, `detail`, `pull`, `sheet`, `hold`).
  - `plotPlanOf` picks the plan kind from the board's archetype, and `filmPlan` times the shots on the beat grid.
  - The sound score is `plot.events`, built by `plotEvents`. `render-api/src/score-audio.ts` reads it through its own `plotScore(tl)`, a different function with the same name.
  - Any change that moves an event changes the music. The blueprint's sound has a line for each run of ink, a letter tick for each piece of the section's and the sheet's lettering, paper moved for the turn and the pull, and the stamp last. The track's first full bar lands on the end of the turn: `plotScore` measures where the track reaches its level after its listed drop and enters that much earlier.
- The board's name and revision come from `boardTitle(board, config)` in `choreo/src/board-title.ts`. The blueprint, trace and montage all use it, so do not change its signature.
  - Name: what the user passes; else the title block's title if it equals the file name, ignoring case; else the file name with `-` and `_` as spaces.
  - Revision: what the user passes; else `silkRevision` (a silk text that is a version on its own, else one after `v` or `rev`, never a bare number); else the title block's `rev`.
  - No other file may read a name or revision on its own.
- Everything comes from the board file. `plot-shots.test.ts` checks that a renamed copy of a board compiles to the identical timeline. No board names and no per-board branches.
- The film is one drawing on one flat sheet, the look the owner approved on 2026-11-12 (`review/approved-1112/blueprint/glasgow-16x9.mp4`). Its rules:
  - Fine ink outlines on cobalt paper. Copper is drawn as outlines (`growing` along each line, `knockout` in the paper's own colour); no filled copper and no heavy strokes from the outline shot to the sheet. No pen, no lamp, no night: a test in `plot-draw.test.ts` fails on any of them.
  - Nothing is erased. A line grows along its length in board units (`growing`), so a camera move never shortens it. Every lettering, once drawn, is drawn on every later frame to the last, at no lower strength. The test "keeps every mark it has drawn" in `plot-draw.test.ts` counts this on what the renderer draws on every frame: fillText kept and never faded, no `opacity()` layer, and no copper line drawn through two views in one frame outside an inset panel. It fails on round 1's cross-fade.
  - The film's body carries no name strip and no board name. The name arrives once, at the end: `drawSheet` letters it large in the drafting face, one glyph after another over `sheetTitleSpan` (f300 to f312 on Glasgow 16:9), on paper the board's drawing does not reach. The stamp letters the revision beside it on the beat the stamp sounds ("REV C3" at f330), in the palette's own accent. A board with no revision gets no REV.
  - Lettering is in the drawing's ink, never on a plate. Every filled rectangle is the paper's colour.
  - The signal is the hero by brightness, not by hue alone. Three weights of line (`plot-draw.ts`): the signal in `signalInk` (the accent lifted half way to white) at `PAIR_GAIN` of the heavy pen; the main chip's fan-out at `FAN_FINE` of `sheetInk` and `FAN_WEIGHT` of the medium pen (`lead` in `PlanOpts`); all other copper at `REST_FINE` and one medium pen. `sheetInk` is the palette's ink mixed 8% toward the paper, so no line or letter is as bright as the signal. Other part bodies draw at `REST_BODIES`; the lines beside the pair in the opening are capped at 0.6 of its pen (`sweepCap`). The test "keeps the signal the brightest and the heaviest line" in `plot-draw.test.ts` reads this off the recorded strokes. Check a change in greyscale (`ffmpeg -vf format=gray`).
- The camera (Glasgow 16:9: pair f0–34, travel f34–150, hold and fan-out f150–210, turn f210–244, parts rising f244–274, pull f274–297, name f300–312, stamp f330, hold to f360):
  - The opening is the pair. The run takes `PAIR_FILL` of the frame's width at the frame's middle on frame 0 (`centred` cancels the stage's lift), then the camera pulls back `PAIR_BACK` of the way to the whole board over a short shot (`SIG_BEATS.pair`, 2.5 beats).
  - A film that opens on its signal has `OPEN_DRAWN` (0.7) of its first lines drawn by frame 0, and the slowest of them still runs to about f22, so frame 0 is a full-weight line in motion across half the frame.
  - Through the pair shot the camera leads with the ink: the head rests `HEAD_AHEAD` (0.2) frame widths ahead of the centre at the end of the macro, and the travel follows it from there. `cook/audit/head-lock.mts <board file> <aspect>` prints the head's place in frame for every frame. It is led, not locked: on Glasgow 16:9 the head drifts from (0.66, 0.43) to (0.48, 0.26) and stops with the camera at f158.
  - A plan with an `outline` shot travels: `travelCam` in `plot-shots.ts` moves straight down (tilt 0) along the yellow signal from the pair to the main chip. The path is a nearest-neighbour chain over the flow points, parametrised by arc length, and smoothed by `wayAt` with a window that shrinks to zero at both ends, so the cut in is clean. The travel keeps the board's long side at least `TRAVEL_HIDE` frame widths, so the whole board is never in frame before the pull (f240 on Glasgow 16:9). It ends with the main chip filling `TRAVEL_HERO` of the frame's height and holds there `TRAVEL_HOLD` beats.
  - A film without a pair opening holds its travel's scale within `TRAVEL_SPAN` of its first frame. A pair film builds instead (`zAlong`): the travel is widest `TRAVEL_WIDE_AT` of the way along, then closes in by `TRAVEL_CLOSER` to land with the chip at `PAIR_HERO` of the stage's height, and its progress runs as time to the power `TRAVEL_BUILD`, so it is fastest near the chip. The still beat before the fan-out stays.
  - `TURN_AT` (14.5) is the latest beat the turn starts in a travel film; `filmPlan` gives the travel every beat up to it that the ending leaves. The turn takes `TURN_BEATS` (2.25 beats, 1.1 s) and its `tilt` event spans it. `objectView` pivots it on the main chip, so the chip holds still while the plan turns into the axonometric drawing; the parts rise in a wave out from the chip (`risen`). The tilt eases in and out once over the whole shot (`easeInOut` in `objectView`); a turn that started at full speed read as a jolt, and an ease laid over an ease did the whole turn in a quarter second. The main chip's own lid is the exception to the wave: it inks over `BODY_DRAW` just before the pull, so the fan-out drawn inside its footprint stays visible through the turn and no slab appears in one frame over fresh ink. A lid drawn as glass over the fan-out was tried and made the chip an unreadable tangle.
  - The turn is one drawing under one camera (`travelled` in `plot-draw.ts`). The object's top copper is the travel's own plan (`travelPlan`, through `ObjectState.plane`), so the copper and the yellow pair are the same ink before, during and after the tilt, and to the last frame. What the turn adds is only added: the parts rise out of their outlines.
  - One continuous pull (`turnKeys`) carries the camera from the chip to the closing view; the name letters as it lands and the stamp is the last event.
- No note, caption or leader states a fact about the copper (round 16; the owner on 2026-11-17: "does anyone really give a fuck about 45 degree bend? like differential pair bro like really?"). The film letters only the main chip's name and its dot, the section's own words, the parts' own markings, and the title sheet's name and stamp. The test "letters no note or caption about the copper" in `plot-draw.test.ts` reads every text the twelve films draw. Do not bring back a bus caption, a ride note, a bend note or a pitch figure.
  - The ink runs at its own pace: nothing waits for lettering (`PlotInk` has no `waits`).
  - The score still places round 15's notes as boxes (`placeText`, `lettering` and `boxes` in `plotScore`), though nothing letters them. Two things read them: the section's held camera (`cut.held`, which keeps every box whole or out of the frame) and the section's "A" marks (`cut.place`). Five of the twelve films frame their held view round those boxes (Glasgow 16:9 and 1:1, iCEBreaker 4:5, the dev kit 4:5 and 9:16). Round 16 kept them so the camera stayed identical; dropping them moves those five held views.
- A travel film draws by an ink clock (`PlotScore.ink`, `inkTime`, `inkClock` in `plot-shots.ts`; `travelPlan`, `inked`, `drawFan` in `plot-draw.ts`): pencil leads the ink by `ink.lead`, the ink spreads from the drafted signal, the camera holds one beat on the chip with nothing drawn (`TRAVEL_HOLD` is 4 beats: one still, three under a slow rise), then the chip's signals leave one after another round the chip, starting at the pin the drafted signal lands on (`ink.fan.from`, `ink.fan.round`; `FAN_ROUND` 1.9 beats round, `FAN_GROW` 1.1 beats out, about 1.5 s in all). The score places its six fan pens across `ink.fan.round`. Three rules keep it from looking at bare paper, found on iCEBreaker and the dev kit:
  - The ink reaches the middle of the frame before the camera does. Where the drafted signal would not, `INK_AHEAD` beats of ink start from the camera's own path. Glasgow needs none, so its film data does not change.
  - A film that travels drops the `open` shot on the chip (`filmPlan`). That shot inked the chip's signals in the first second; the travel then showed the chip without them until the fan-out.
  - A bus shot that opens the film starts 40% into its strokes, so frame 0 is a drawing in progress.
  - Measure it: the share of pixels brighter than the paper, per frame, must never sit near zero for a run of frames. A board without a travel (StickHub) keeps the older film.
- On a film without a travel, the yellow trail and the parts' standing times follow the route the wave rides (`drawWave`).
  - `thinTrail` thins only the drawn trail; the route geometry the camera follows stays whole, so thinning never moves the camera.
  - A board with a trail tells one story: `plotScore` reruns with `signal = true`, which gives no power stations and so no `detail` shot.
- The bus rule: `CHIP` is `/^(U|IC)\d/i`, so resistor networks are not chips. When a route stop is reached only through two or more chips, the route follows the main part's widest connector bus instead, and the sentence becomes "N SIGNALS FROM <bus> TO <hero>." A test in `plot-shots.test.ts` pins Glasgow's and the dev kit's routes and sentences.
- The ending is the tilted sheet seen low and close (`closeUp` in `plot-shots.ts`), not a title sheet: no specification table, no panels, no title block.
  - The view is `sheet.iso`, at the elevation `CLOSE_E` (26°). `closeUp` takes the largest scale from `CLOSE_OVER` (times a whole-board fit) at which a place for the main chip leaves the name and the stamp on clear paper and keeps the chip's top in frame; among those it takes the most board in frame. On most boards the far edge leaves the frame. A valve stands too tall for that and gets a whole view.
  - `nameSet` sets the name: one line up to 20% of the short side and 60% of the width, less the stamp's room, else two lines broken between words. `sheet.name` carries its place and the stamp's.
  - From the beat the sheet lands the board's drawing grows 6% about its main chip (`SHEET_PUSH`, in `objectView`), so the hold moves. The name and the stamp do not move.
  - `layoutSheet`, the panel rectangles and the callouts are still computed in the score and no longer drawn. Clear them once the owner has judged this ending.
- The turn and the sheet use different angles and scales (`object.psi`, `object.scale`), blended over `object.turn`.
  - The camera's `viewOf` in `plot-shots.ts` and `objectView` in `plot-draw.ts` must blend over the same window. Change one without the other and the board pulls back small, then turns.
- Markings: the hero's lid text is drawn with `keep` (`lidText` in `plot-body.ts`). It shrinks to fit but never below `0.2 × L0`, so it stays readable to the last frame.
- No blank frame after a cut: an outline that starts on a cut begins `CUT_HEAD` beats into its drawing and eases out (`plot-draw.ts`).
- The fast look-loop for stills is `npx tsx scripts/styleframes.mts --style plot --boards glasgow,icebreaker --aspect 4:5 --at 0.2,0.6,1`, about 20 s a board.
  - Run the tests alone with `npx vitest run packages/choreo/src/trace/plot-shots.test.ts packages/render/src/plot-draw.test.ts packages/choreo/src/board-title.test.ts`. That takes about six minutes; run it again together with `packages/choreo/src/trace`.
  - To prove other films did not change, hash `JSON.stringify(compileTimeline(...))` before and after the change.
