# Trace style: what a change must keep

Read this before you change `packages/choreo/src/trace/` (except `plot-shots.ts`) or `packages/render/src/trace-*.ts`. The root `AGENTS.md` points here.

- Files: `choreo/src/trace/scene.ts` builds the whole film (`buildTraceScene`, part lifts in `entryLift`); `shots.ts` solves the camera (`cineShots`). `camera.ts` has the camera math, sampling and `heroOcclusion`. `lockup.ts` sets the poster's words. `nets.ts` assigns net roles, `hero.ts` picks the main part, `archetype.ts` reads the board's character, and `parts.ts` estimates part bodies. In `render/src`, `trace-gl.ts` is the lit renderer, `trace-maps.ts` builds its data maps, and `trace-draw.ts` is the flat fallback.
- The arc is 360 frames on a 24-beat grid (`TRACE_ACTS`, `actsFor`). The copper draws, the coat goes on, parts land, the board breathes, power comes on, and the camera settles low on the main chip under the board's name. The main chip's marking is hidden until the wake (`bareLid` in `buildTraceScene`), then the `hero` light finds it. The sound is `render-api/src/score-audio.ts`, placed on the scene's events: moving a cut, the light-in or the wake moves the sound.
- The camera is `cineShots` in `choreo/src/trace/shots.ts`. Six takes, low (tilt 60° or more from the vertical, 54° in a tall frame) except one look straight down; `grammar.test.ts` fails a film that looks from above or shows the whole board anywhere but the coat take and that look, or ends anywhere but a held low frame of the main chip. The owner found the film with no such look repetitive (15 November: "the middle and near the ending like it feels a bit repetitive"); no stretch of more than about two seconds may hold one subject from similar views. The trace film's own takes sit behind `film` (`resolved.style === 'trace'`); montage and glyph solve through the same function and keep the older coat take and straight-down ending. After a change here, hash `buildTraceScene` for styles `montage` and `glyph` before and after; they must not move.
  1. Spark, frame 0 to the beat the mask starts in. Black. Tilt 80°, 14° lens (nearer in a tall frame, up to 1.6× in 9:16, so lit copper runs from the bottom edge past the middle), trucking at least 1.6 half-frames along the direction most of the early copper runs from the opening part (length- and distance-weighted, so every aspect picks the same heading; a tall frame turns up to 20° toward depth). The trace film focuses between the look-at and the growth front, stops down (`FRONT`) and shifts the picture up so the copper fills the top of the frame.
  2. Coat, from beat 5 (dense boards draw for 3 beats, `actsFor(..., COAT_BEATS*B)`) to the silk. In the trace film this is the one look at the whole board: tilt 62° (54° in 9:16, `WHOLE_TALL`), 30° lens, the outline fitted inside the frame from the three-quarter heading that fills it most (`WHOLE_TURN` off the board's axes), light `sheen` (a low side key and a softbox whose reflection travels across the coat). It is exempt from `withhold`. Montage and glyph keep the close coat take: tilt 77°, 24° lens, one beat.
  3. Skyline, to beat 12. Tilt 82° (down to 74° in a 9:16 frame, so the board's depth fills a tall frame), 14° lens on the main chip, which spans 28% of a 16:9 frame's width, rising to 42% in a square frame and 50% in a tall one. The heading is searched in 10° steps: tall parts behind the chip score, cover of the chip (`heroOcclusion`) and a lens inside a part cost. Light `skyline`: the parts land on the lit board in its own colour, the only bright stretch before the power-on. Then `rim` from the main chip's landing (the breath).
  4. Ride, beat 12 to beat 16: one take with no cut, light `ride` (a cool low key with enough fill that dark parts read as objects). Montage's trailer cut reuses this take (its f201–235 samples these shots, this light and these energies), so a ride change moves the montage too. `flyway` plans a course on a grid by Dijkstra from the chip: parts taller than 0.7 of the flying height are walls, large flat parts are walls too, and the cost prefers cells near followed copper and away from crowding. The start sits 1 to 2.5 chip-reach out (`RIDE_REACH`) inside a 35° cone opposite the arrival, the power entry preferred. The lens opens at 38° and tightens toward the arrival (`RIDE_TIGHTEN`); the arrival heading, tilt and stand-off are searched over 24 yaws × 3 tilts for cover, foreground, posts and room behind, and the stand-off is set by the room, so the arrival lens ends near 16–34°, not 12°. `chaseLight` in `scene.ts` retimes copper near the course so its light runs ahead of the camera and reaches the chip at the wake (`WAKE`, beat 15); copper already behind the front is lit whole on the cut, and the lit lane widens where the ride flies higher. Along the ride the camera rises (up to `RIDE_RISE` × its flying height) wherever one part nearer than what it looks at would cover more than `RIDE_WALL` of the frame (`nearCover` in `camera.ts`); a start that stays walled at full rise is skipped if the course keeps 0.85 of `RIDE_REACH`. The solver flies each of its candidate arrivals and keeps the first unless another has fewer flawed samples (a wall, or under `RIDE_SEEN` frame widths of chased copper in sight). Then `sightFlaws` measures the kept ride the way `grammar.test.ts`'s `rideSight` does: it lights the copper with `chaseLight` and, over the ride's first 45 frames, counts frames with under a fifth of a frame width of lit copper in sight past the parts, and frames past a four-frame run behind one part covering a quarter of the frame. A ride with any such frame flies the other arrivals in `RIDE_VARIANTS`: skip 15% of the course, look toward the main chip instead of the stand point, start the light a look ahead. It keeps the first with none, else the fewest, and only among rides whose camera still travels a third of the board's longer side (`RIDE_RUN`). This is what fixed iCEBreaker 9:16 and the dev kit 4:5 without their models, and iCEBreaker 4:5 and 9:16 and the dev kit 9:16 with them. A tall frame's arrival stands far off (the chip must span most of a narrow frame), so on a 2:1 board the cone search alone flies along the board's edge. The trace-gl `rim` light flashes on the hero's landing (`SEAT_FLASH`).
  5. The ending, trace film: two cuts. The light turns to `hero` at the wake and the ride's arrival lingers on the lid for two beats while the highlight crosses it (`LID_CROSSED`). Then a cut straight down on the lit board (light `power`), which pulls back from 2.4× to the whole outline in two beats (`WHOLE_LOOK`, `WHOLE_LOOK_FROM`); the LEDs come on in it. Then a cut to the last frame, 14% closer and 7° round (`HERO_IN`, `HERO_SWING`), which settles in one beat and holds from beat 20 (`HERO_FROM`): tilt 76° (14° above the board), 22° lens, the chip a tenth of the frame wider than the skyline holds it, its top edge just above the middle (`HERO_TOP`), a tall part held smaller so it fits (`HERO_FLOOR`). The heading is the marking's square-on view nearest the arrival, turned 14°, 24° or 36° either way (`HERO_OFF`), scored for parts behind the chip, cover of the chip, and one near part filling the foreground (`nearCover`). The held frame pushes in 4% (`HERO_PUSH`). Montage and glyph keep the cut to straight down at frame 240 and the pull back to a poster (`PULL_RUN`, `PULL_REACH`, `HOLD_FROM`).
- The held last frame is not still. The solver pushes it 4%, the renderer pushes it 1.5% more (`POSTER_PUSH` in `handheld`, `trace-gl.ts`) and one LED blinks (`liveLed`, the `blink` uniform), both from `acts.hold[0]`. The renderer's two are not in the timeline. A trace film has no `poster` key, so `boardGain`'s exposure probe and the crane's rig blend do not run for it. Part shadows on the fx map are swept shapes, not stacked rectangles (`trace-maps.ts`).
- `grammar.test.ts` also asks (`rideSight`), over the ride's first 45 frames, that lit copper in sight past every part box spans a fifth of the frame's width every second frame and that no one part nearer than the lens's target covers a quarter of the frame for more than four frames. It fails round 2's saved Glasgow 4:5 timeline (16-frame wall, 11 dark samples). Measure the picture too: a geometric pass can still look dark (`review/cine-trace-r03/tools/stills.mts` renders draft stills, `luma.py` tabulates them).
- `grammar.test.ts` also asks, for three dense boards in four aspects: the ride travels a third of the board's longer side by the wake and tightens; the last frame holds 1.5–2.2 s; no frame is dark (lit copper spans 40% of the width outside the coat-to-power window, the board covers 20% of the frame cells inside it, which is why a tall frame's whole-board take looks further down).
- Every key before the ending pushes in until the board's outline overflows the frame by 15% (`withhold`), except the trace film's coat take; from the skyline on, keys pull back until the main chip's box sits inside 95% of the frame (`keepHero`); a low key rises (to 68° at most) until its lens clears every part (`clearLens`).
- Parts: nothing appears from nothing. Each part's fall is a straight, eased path from a height and a plane offset (`entryPath`, `TracePart.enter`) chosen so its first drawn frame is wholly outside the frame and its first visible frame shows at most `ENTRY_SLIVER` (half) of it at an edge. A part may come in through any edge (down the screen is preferred) and may lengthen its fall to one beat; landing times never move. In a close, steep shot a straight drop cannot start outside the frame without passing the lens, which is why the path slides. A falling part never covers more than `ENTRY_COVER` (30%) of the frame, or more than it covers at rest; a box header once fell through the lens and gave four black frames. It lands once, square, and never moves again. `entrance.test.ts` checks every part of the seven set films.
- Lit lines lighting after the wake stay dark when more than `LIT_CROWD` (30%) of their length runs within one width of longer lines. This keeps a dense board from becoming haze at phone size.
- Words, trace film: the board's name in heavy caps (`TraceLockup.face: 'grotesk'`, SilkGrotesk 900, drawn by `drawLockup` in `render/src/title.ts`) above the main chip, the revision small after it in the same face (weight 600, caps), nothing else. A long name wraps at its words to at most three lines and takes the wrap that sets it largest. The name is built letter by letter (`lockup.build`); the build's start, each word's landing and the frame the name reads whole are the sound's events. The marking flips 180° (`turn`) so it reads upright at the last frame's heading; the silk lies as the board has it. Montage and glyph keep the mono poster block: straight down, the name below or in a column beside the board (`titleBlock`, `lockupAt`).
- A main part on the back copper flips the whole film to the back side (`back` in `buildTraceScene`, mirrored in x).
- Look-loop: `npx tsx scripts/trace-frames.mts <board> <aspect> 0,105,180,359 <dir>` renders final-quality stills. Each run leaves a 37 MB `remotion-webpack-bundle-*` in `$TMPDIR`; delete it. Full films go through `npm run reel -- … --title … --version …` at `SILK_REEL_CONCURRENCY=4`, about 4 min each.
- Tests: run each file in `packages/choreo/src/trace/` alone (`npx vitest run packages/choreo/src/trace/<name>.test.ts`), and `packages/render/src/landing.test.ts`, which checks fall lengths and that parts never move after landing.
- The part pose has three mirrors that must agree: `drop()` in trace-gl's `LANDING` (the hero eases out, other parts ease in and out), `partDrop`/`partPose` in `trace-maps.ts` (also the shadows), and the projection in `entryPath` and `entrance.test.ts`. The whole set takes about 20 min one file at a time; `aspect`, `grammar` and `lockup` are the slowest. Under machine load, `silk` and `trace-draw` can time out at 5 s and pass on a rerun. To prove other films did not change, hash `JSON.stringify(compileTimeline(...))` for each before and after.
- Traps:
  - `montage-hype.ts` clones its timeline with `style: 'trace'` to draw through `trace-gl.ts`, so a renderer branch on `tl.style === 'trace'` also runs for montage. Key trace-only looks on data: the light names `sheen`, `land` and `hero`, and the rig fields `partEnv`, `heroLit`, `rimAz` and `etch`, whose absent values keep the old picture.
  - `fallHeight` in `trace-gl.ts` finds the place take by its light (`place` or `skyline`). A new light name on that take must be added there or every part's fall shrinks to the least height.
  - A seated body stands on the board. The board shader reads the part-shadow channel (`fx.g`): inside a footprint it darkens the board and lets no net glow out (`under`, `seat`), and `glowOn` gives a body's underside none. The owner hates a chip that floats on a bright gap, in any style; check frames 240 and 359 at native size after any change to glow or shadows.
  - An IC's epoxy takes half the studio's light (`LID` in `MARKING`); its marking takes all of it. The main chip is revealed by a highlight that crosses the lid after the wake (`Rig.sweep`, `SWEEP_*`), not by the whole lid turning grey.
  - In the `hero` rig `partRoom` is absolute, not a multiple of `E`. `E` falls with a pale mask, so a multiple lit a dark board's chip nearly white (iCEBreaker).
  - A crystal drawn from its STEP model is a satin can in `FS_MESH` (metal 0.7, roughness 0.5) and skips the plated-lead mirror; as a mirror, metal has no diffuse light and the can reflects the dark studio as a black slab.
  - Scene probes without `loadPartBodies` miss STEP models: a header's tall pins exist only in the model, so occlusion checks must load bodies the way the CLI does.
  - `litRead` counts copper within about 1.8 mm, so it calls a frame full that looks empty; count rows by exact hits.
  - In a 4:5 frame, H/W is 1.25, so any tall-frame rule with a cutoff below that changes 4:5 films too.
  - Corner-only tests miss a part that spans the whole frame.
  - `withhold` checks every outline vertex: sampling every fourth missed a rectangle (four vertices) and dove the dev kit's 4:5 arrival to 0.1 mm.
  - Other builders' scripts delete `remotion-webpack-bundle-*` in `$TMPDIR`; a still script that bundles there can lose its bundle mid-run. Bundle into your own folder (`outDir`).
  - `silk.test.ts` builds the dev kit scene inside a 5 s test, and the composer and film tests compile timelines for every board; a scene must build well under a second. Keep the ride solver's work bounded. `nearCover` drops boxes outside the frustum and tests each ray's boxes nearest first; a flight is memoised per arrival, skip and aim (the early light only moves the front); `flaws` is lazy; `flyGrid` is built once per board; `cineShots` memoises the last eight solves by their inputs. Every speed-up must leave the shots bit-identical: hash the scene for all boards and aspects before and after (`review/cine-trace-r04/tools/time2.mts` prints hashes and times).
